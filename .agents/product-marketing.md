## 1. Product Overview

**One-liner:** Awaaz Labs is the AI front desk that captures every inbound lead an appointment-based business would otherwise miss, then books, reminds, follows up, and collects the review.

**What it does:** Awaaz Labs plugs into the channels a business already uses (phone, WhatsApp, SMS, email, landing page forms) and answers every inbound inquiry instantly, 24/7, in the customer's language. It qualifies the lead, books the appointment directly into the calendar, sends confirmations and reminders, calls no-shows back, reactivates lapsed customers, and requests Google reviews after the visit. Every interaction is transcribed and scored by Qualicall.

**Category (the shelf customers search on):** Missed call recovery / AI receptionist / lead capture and appointment booking automation. NOT "voice AI platform" and NOT "conversational AI." Those are delivery mechanisms and never appear in a headline.

**Product type:** Managed SaaS (done-for-you build, monthly subscription on combined minutes).

**Business model:** One-time setup per agent + monthly combined-minute plans (Essential / Growth / Scale / Enterprise). Priced per market (AED / SAR / QAR).

**Hard rule: no pricing anywhere on the public site.** No figures, tiers, or currency amounts. Pricing is discussed on the sales call only.

**Universal-base rule:** base pages are globally universal (no currency, no named regulators, no city names, no region-specific language lists). Regional specificity lives only on city pages ({base}/{city}).

## 3. Problems and Pain Points

- Industry benchmark: appointment-based businesses miss 23 to 42 percent of inbound calls. In aesthetics, 40 to 60 percent of inquiries arrive after hours.
- 78 percent of after-hours callers never leave a voicemail. They book with the next business on the list.
- WhatsApp inquiries sit unread overnight; leads go cold in minutes, not days.
- No-shows quietly drain calendar revenue; nobody calls them back.
- Owners cannot see what happens on calls. Quality varies by staff member and day.
- Cost of the alternative: a fully loaded receptionist covers ~40 of 168 weekly hours, one language, one call at a time.

**Emotional tension:** The owner knows money is leaking but cannot see where. Every unanswered ring is a competitor's new customer.

## 4. Positioning (locked rules)

- **Outcome first, always.** Headlines sell captured revenue, booked appointments, full calendars. "AI," "agentic," and "voice AI" appear only in supporting copy as the how, never the what.
- **Defensible claims only.** Named proof: Sirius Solutions Global (US, live in production), Advanzatech (UAE, 70 meetings booked in month two of deployment), Telecom Foundation (Pakistan Ministry of IT subsidiary). Benchmark stats are cited as industry data, never as Awaaz results. No invented testimonials, counters, or badges. Ever.
- **Coverage framing, not replacement framing.** Awaaz Labs covers the 76 percent of the week the front desk cannot. It does not replace staff.
- **The system, not a chatbot.** Position as a complete inbound operating system (capture → qualify → book → remind → recover → review → QA), which no single point tool matches.

## 5. Differentiation

| Alternative | Why it falls short |
|---|---|
| Hire more front desk staff | 40 hrs/week, one call at a time, one or two languages, cost scales linearly |
| Voicemail / IVR | 78 percent of callers hang up; captures nothing |
| Chatbot widgets | One channel, no booking, no voice, no follow-through |
| Answering services | Take messages; do not book, remind, recover, or collect reviews |
| Point tools (booking app + reminder app + review app) | Fragmented, nothing owns the lead end to end |

**Key differentiator sentence:** The only system that owns the entire inbound journey, from first ring to five-star review.

## 7. Voice and Style

- Direct, confident, numerate. Talks money, not technology.
- Short sentences. Active voice. No exclamation points.
- No em dashes anywhere. Use periods, commas, or colons.
- No buzzwords: never "streamline," "optimize," "innovative," "revolutionize," "cutting-edge."
- Rhetorical questions allowed sparingly ("How many calls did you miss last night?").
